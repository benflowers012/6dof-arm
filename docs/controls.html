Xbox controller plugged into the laptop, with python arm.py running.

Moving the arm

The sticks and triggers set speed, not position. Push a little and the joint creeps; push all the way and it moves at about 60° per second. Let go and the joint holds where it is. Every joint stops at its safe limit on its own.

ControlMotor
Left stick ↔Base (turns the whole arm)
Left stick ↕Shoulder (lifts the arm)
Right stick ↕Elbow
Right stick ↔Wrist (tilts the claw)
LB / RBTwist the claw left / right
LT / RTClose / open the claw
ButtonWhat it does
ARun the flip: grab in front, swing over the top, set it down behind
BStop. Cancels whatever is playing; the arm holds still
XStart recording; press again to stop and save
YReplay the most recent recording
MenuGo to the home pose
ViewPrint the current angles in the terminal (for tuning)

Good to know

  • Touching a stick also stops anything that's playing, the same as B.
  • Before a recording, the flip, or home starts, the arm first eases from wherever it is (about 1.5 s), so it never jumps.
  • Recordings save to laptop/recordings/. Y always plays the newest one.
  • A recording shorter than a blink is thrown away ("recording too short").
  • Unplug the controller and the program stops; the arm freezes where it is.
  • Stick moves a joint the wrong way, or a button does the wrong thing? That's a one-line change at the top of laptop/arm.py. See the user guide.
