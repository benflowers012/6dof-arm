Robot arm wiring

6× MG996R servos · Arduino Uno · separate servo power supply

Never power the servos from the Uno's 5V pin. Six MG996Rs can pull over 10 A together. The Uno gets its power from the laptop's USB cable and only sends the signal wires.

A dot means the wires connect. Wires that cross without a dot don't touch.

Servo connections

Each MG996R has three wires: brown = ground, red = power, orange = signal.

#JointOrange → Uno pinRed →Brown →

Wiring steps

  1. Build the two rails (a terminal block or a strip of perfboard works). Use thick wire, around 18 AWG, from the supply to the rails.
  2. Connect the supply's + to the +6 V rail and its − to the GND rail.
  3. Solder the 1000 µF capacitor across the rails. Its stripe (−) leg goes to GND. Use one rated for 16 V or higher.
  4. Plug in each servo: red to +6 V, brown to GND, orange to its Uno pin from the table.
  5. Run one wire from any Uno GND pin to the GND rail. Without this shared ground the servos twitch at random.
  6. Connect the Uno to the laptop over USB. Turn on the servo supply last.

Before the first power-on

  • Upload the firmware before turning on the servo supply, so the arm goes to its home pose rather than wherever old code sends it.
  • Check with a meter that the rails aren't shorted together.
  • Keep a hand near the supply's switch the first time. If anything grinds or buzzes hard, cut power and tighten that joint's limits.
