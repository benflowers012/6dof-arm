Robot arm wiring

6× MG996R · Adafruit 16-channel servo driver (PCA9685) · Arduino Uno R3 · 5 V 10 A supply

Servo power never goes through the Uno. The 5 V 10 A supply connects only to the driver board's blue screw terminal. The Uno's 5V pin powers only the driver's chip (its VCC pin).

A dot means the wires connect. Each servo cable is drawn as its three colored wires.

Wiring steps

  1. Screw the barrel adapter's + and − to the driver's blue terminal: + to V+, − to GND. Use 18–20 AWG wire. Jumper wires are too thin for 10 A.
  2. Capacitor (the little can marked 1000 µF): it smooths the sudden current spikes when servos start moving, so the voltage doesn't dip. Put its striped leg in GND and the other leg in V+. Either clamp both legs into the blue terminal with the power wires, or solder it into the spot on the board labeled C2. Backwards, it can pop.
  3. Plug the servos into channels 0–5 (use the extension cables where the wires don't reach up the arm).
  4. Wire the four jumpers from the Uno to the driver's side header (table above).
  5. Connect the Uno to the laptop with the USB cable. Plug in the 5 V supply last.

Before the first power-on

  • Upload the firmware before plugging in the 5 V supply, so the arm goes to its home pose rather than doing whatever old code says.
  • Check with a meter that V+ and GND aren't shorted.
  • Keep a hand on the 5 V plug the first time. If anything grinds or buzzes hard, unplug it and tighten that joint's limits.
